01

The question

Breast cancer intrinsic subtypes are often presented as portable biology. Replica tests that claim the way an external audit should: freeze the published calls, lock the model, move it to an independent cohort, and ask whether it still adds information beyond age, stage, and receptor status.

The audit compares the published PAM50/Claudin-low calls in TCGA-BRCA (reference cohort) and METABRIC (external validation). It reports the subtype association, the incremental value over a clinical baseline, and a censoring-aware nonparametric combination across OS and DFS/RFS. IntClust is used as an internal METABRIC benchmark because no external IntClust cohort exists.

The replication rule was not met across both OS cohorts: one cohort separated and the other did not. Incremental value was not established under the prespecified ΔC rule.

07

External transport

The TCGA clinical + PAM50 model is locked and applied to METABRIC. Discrimination uses risk ranking; calibration at 60 months exposes baseline-hazard differences.

What the transport test asks

A model can rank patients well and still be miscalibrated when the baseline hazard differs. Replica reports both instead of collapsing them into one number.

Discrimination. Clinical 0.632 → clinical + PAM50 0.640; ΔC +0.008 [+0.0001, +0.0151]. The interval only just excludes zero.

Locked model: age per 10 years, stage III/IV, ER+, PR+, and subtype. No METABRIC outcome information enters the coefficients.

Calibration slope: 0.71. A value near 1 means the locked linear predictor transfers on the log-hazard scale; a value below 1 indicates over-dispersion.

METABRIC internal benchmark: clinical 0.630 → +PAM50 0.634 → +PAM50+IntClust 0.640; IntClust adds +0.0056 over PAM50. This is internal, not external validation.

08

Nonparametric combination

The four component tests are dependent. NPC calibrates the combined statistic against the joint permutation distribution instead of assuming independence.

Why not Bonferroni?

Bonferroni controls the family-wise error rate but is conservative when tests are positively dependent. NPC preserves the empirical dependence and can detect a consistent moderate signal across OS and DFS/RFS.

The global p is an association test, not a replication test. It is driven by the two METABRIC components (both at the 0.0005 floor); the TCGA components are 0.307 and 0.495. A significant global test does not rescue the cohort-specific decision.

Permutation scheme: bivariate outcome pairs are permuted jointly within cohort, so censoring and the OS–DFS/RFS correlation are preserved. The global null uses 2,000 permutations.

What would change the answer

Threats to validity

PAM50 calls are not assay-identical across cohorts. TCGA labels come from a published classifier; METABRIC labels come from a different pipeline. TCGA grade is absent from the cBioPortal export, so the primary clinical baseline uses age, stage, ER, and PR; grade is a METABRIC-only sensitivity. DFS and RFS are not the same endpoint. Complete-case analysis can bias estimates if missingness is informative.

What the audit does not claim

Replica is not a causal analysis, not a new classifier, and not a clinical decision tool. A subtype coefficient is an association under the fitted model. The permutation test addresses exchangeability, not confounding. The dashboard is a communication layer over frozen artifacts, not a live patient-facing prognostic device.
